# Naming Scorecard and Test Protocol

Use this reference for Develop, Audit, and finalist comparison work.

## Weighted scorecard

Score each criterion from 1 to 5, multiply by the weight, and normalize to 100.

| Criterion | Weight | What to examine |
|---|---:|---|
| Strategic fit | 20 | Alignment with the promise, positioning, and intended character |
| Pronunciation | 15 | Ease after one reading across target languages |
| Memorability | 15 | Distinct sound shape and recall after a delay |
| Distinctiveness | 15 | Separation from competitors and generic category vocabulary |
| Global usability | 10 | Cross-language spelling, sound, and negative-meaning risk |
| Extensibility | 10 | Ability to host future products, sectors, and markets |
| Hear-to-spell accuracy | 5 | Likelihood that a listener writes the intended form |
| Visual potential | 5 | Word shape, logo usability, and typographic balance |
| Architecture fit | 5 | Natural use with parent, product, module, and endorsement forms |

Compute:

`normalized score = sum(rating × weight) / 5`

Interpretation:

- **85–100:** exceptional finalist;
- **75–84:** strong candidate;
- **65–74:** promising but needs a clear reason to retain;
- **below 65:** remove from the main shortlist.

Do not let a high score override a hard validation failure.

## Validation gates

Assign one status to every finalist:

- **Pass:** no material issue found in the completed screening layers;
- **Hold:** unresolved pronunciation, meaning, market, domain, registry, or trademark concern;
- **Reject:** direct category collision, harmful meaning, severe confusion, or architecture failure.

Legal availability is unresolved until the relevant official registers, territories, and trademark classes have been checked. Even a Pass remains preliminary unless qualified counsel provides clearance.

## Practical tests

Apply the tests appropriate to the decision stage:

1. **Crowded Bar Test:** Can it be heard, understood, and remembered over the noise of a crowded bar?
2. **Phonological Loop Test:** Does it use alliteration or rhyme to stick in short-term verbal memory?
3. **Phone test:** Say it once; can the listener spell it correctly without asking for clarification?
4. **Delayed recall:** Can the listener remember it after ten minutes?
5. **Sentence test:** Does it sound natural in a customer or press sentence?
6. **Architecture test:** Does `<Product> by <Company>` sound credible?
7. **Expansion test:** Could the company launch an unrelated second product under it?
8. **Global reading test:** Do target-language speakers converge on a usable pronunciation?
9. **Search test:** Can users find it on Google without adding category descriptors (Own the search)?
10. **Aging test:** Is it likely to survive a ten-year technology shift?
11. **Separation test:** Does it remain distinguishable when competitor names are read beside it?

When real user testing is unavailable, clearly label the assessment as an expert prediction rather than observed recall or pronunciation data.

## Tie-breaking order

When finalists score closely, prefer in this order:

1. fewer validation risks;
2. stronger strategic fit;
3. easier pronunciation and hear-to-spell performance;
4. greater category distinctiveness;
5. better long-term extensibility;
6. domain preference.

Do not choose a strategically weaker name solely because an exact `.com` appears free.
