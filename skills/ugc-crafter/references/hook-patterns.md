# Hook Patterns — First 3 Seconds

The hook determines whether someone stops scrolling. Every UGC video lives or dies in its opening frame.

## Hook Categories

### 1. Pattern Interrupt

Break the viewer's scrolling rhythm with something unexpected.

**Techniques:**
- Mid-action start (already doing something when clip begins)
- Unusual camera angle (extreme close-up, upside-down, from behind)
- Contrasting elements (clean hands holding something dirty, luxury item in mundane setting)

**Prompt patterns:**
```
"Close-up of hands already mid-action, [specific action], camera slightly tilted,
smartphone front-camera perspective"

"Extreme close-up of [product detail] filling 80% of frame, slowly pulling back
to reveal the character holding it"

"Camera starts on [unexpected detail] then pans up to reveal character's face
with [expression]"
```

---

### 2. Direct Address

Character speaks directly to the viewer, creating immediate personal connection.

**Techniques:**
- Start mid-sentence (feels like joining a conversation)
- Lean-in whisper (creates intimacy)
- Pointed finger at camera (YOU need to hear this)

**Prompt patterns:**
```
"Character leaning slightly toward camera, index finger raised as if making
a point, mouth open mid-word, direct eye contact with lens"

"Extreme closeness to camera, character whispering with wide eyes as if
sharing a secret, hand partially covering mouth"

"Character pointing directly at camera lens with serious expression,
eyebrows slightly raised, as if catching the viewer's attention"
```

---

### 3. Visual Proof

Lead with the result before the story.

**Techniques:**
- Show the "after" first
- Product result in frame from the start
- Side-by-side comparison visible immediately

**Prompt patterns:**
```
"Close-up of [impressive result] taking up full frame, character's hand
entering from the side holding the product that created it"

"Split composition: left side shows [before state], right side shows
[after state], character standing between them pointing at the difference"

"Character holding [result] up to camera with amazed expression,
product visible on the table behind them"
```

---

### 4. Curiosity Gap

Create a question the viewer needs answered.

**Techniques:**
- Show a strange or unexpected situation
- Character reacting to something off-screen
- Text-worthy facial expression without context

**Prompt patterns:**
```
"Character staring at something below the camera with confused expression,
one eyebrow raised, head tilted, holding [product] loosely in one hand"

"Character looking shocked, mouth slightly open, hand covering cheek,
looking down at [product/result] on the table"

"Hands holding [product] in frame from above, one hand gesturing
'wait for it' with index finger, face not visible yet"
```

---

### 5. Relatable Moment

Start with something the viewer has experienced.

**Techniques:**
- Universal frustration (tangled cables, messy desk, bad hair day)
- Common routine moment (morning coffee, getting ready)
- Shared experience (scrolling phone, looking at mirror)

**Prompt patterns:**
```
"Character sitting on unmade bed, hair messy, scrolling phone with
slightly bored expression, natural morning lighting through window"

"Character standing in front of bathroom mirror, examining skin closely,
slight frown, natural overhead lighting, relatable morning routine"

"Character at cluttered desk, sighing while looking at [the problem],
shoulders slightly slumped, everyday office lighting"
```

---

### 6. Product Reveal

The product itself is the hook.

**Techniques:**
- Aesthetic unboxing moment
- Product in motion (pouring, opening, transforming)
- Satisfying product interaction (click, snap, texture)

**Prompt patterns:**
```
"Hands slowly removing product from minimal packaging, close-up angle,
natural soft lighting, focus on tactile moment of first touch"

"Product being poured/squeezed/opened in extreme close-up, satisfying
texture or movement visible, blurred background"

"Fingers pressing/clicking product mechanism, macro-level detail,
satisfying physical interaction moment"
```

## Hook Selection by Archetype

| GRWM | Relatable Moment | Product Reveal |

## Hook Anti-Patterns

These kill authenticity in UGC hooks:

- ❌ Perfect center-frame composition (too staged)
- ❌ Character standing still and smiling at camera (stock photo energy)
- ❌ Starting from a black screen or fade-in (too produced)
- ❌ Wide establishing shot (too cinematic)
- ❌ Character waving at camera (amateur but wrong kind of amateur)
- ❌ Product placed perfectly on a styled surface with no human element

## Hook Evaluation Criteria

Score every hook prompt on:

1. **Stop Power (1-10):** Would this make someone pause scrolling?
2. **Context Speed (1-10):** How fast does the viewer understand what this is about?
3. **Authenticity (1-10):** Does this feel like something a real person would post?
4. **Curiosity (1-10):** Does this create a "need to know more" feeling?

Minimum passing score: 7 average across all four criteria.
