# UGC Video Archetypes

Eight proven UGC content structures. Select the archetype before writing any prompt.

## 1. Problem → Solution

**Structure:** Show the pain → Introduce relief → Demonstrate the fix

**Best for:** Skincare, cleaning products, productivity tools, health supplements

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: Character visibly frustrated with the problem (close-up of the struggle)
2. PROBLEM: Quick demonstration of why current solutions fail
3. PIVOT: "Then I found this..." moment — product enters frame
4. SOLUTION: Product in action, solving the exact problem shown earlier
5. CTA: Satisfied expression + product close-up

**Emotional arc:** Frustration → curiosity → relief → satisfaction

**Camera notes:** Tighter framing during problem scenes, slightly wider during solution. The shift in framing subconsciously signals change.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Visibly struggling with [problem], slight frown, furrowed brow"
- "Eyes light up with genuine surprise as they notice the difference"
- "Relaxed posture and natural smile while demonstrating the result"

---

## 2. Testimonial

**Structure:** Direct-to-camera honest review

**Best for:** Services, subscription products, high-trust purchases

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: Mid-sentence start — feels like joining a conversation already happening
2. STORY: Personal context for why they tried the product
3. EXPERIENCE: Specific details about using it (not generic praise)
4. VERDICT: Honest assessment with one minor qualifier for credibility
5. CTA: Casual recommendation, not hard sell

**Emotional arc:** Approachable → credible → convincing

**Camera notes:** Single continuous take. Eye contact with camera. Slight lean-in during key points. Natural pauses between thoughts.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Speaking directly to camera with relaxed, conversational tone"
- "Slight head tilt and brief pause, gathering thoughts naturally"
- "Genuine micro-expressions — not rehearsed, not performative"

---

## 3. Unboxing

**Structure:** Package reveal → First impressions → Initial use

**Best for:** Electronics, premium products, subscription boxes, beauty products

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: Sealed package on table, hands entering frame
2. REVEAL: Opening process, slow enough to build anticipation
3. FIRST LOOK: Genuine reaction to seeing the product
4. TOUCH: Tactile exploration — texture, weight, quality signals
5. CTA: First use moment or product displayed beautifully

**Emotional arc:** Anticipation → excitement → satisfaction

**Camera notes:** Top-down POV for opening, transition to front-facing for reactions. Hands always in frame. Package sounds are important context cues.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Hands carefully opening packaging, fingers tracing the seal"
- "Genuinely surprised expression, eyebrows raised, slight gasp"
- "Turning product in hands, examining details, nodding approvingly"

---

## 4. Before/After

**Structure:** Visual proof of transformation

**Best for:** Skincare, fitness, cleaning, organization, home improvement

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: The "before" state — visually compelling problem
2. BEFORE: Clear documentation of starting condition
3. PROCESS: Brief product application or usage
4. AFTER: Same angle, same lighting — dramatic difference
5. CTA: Side-by-side reaction or close-up of result

**Emotional arc:** Dissatisfaction → effort → amazement

**Camera notes:** Identical framing for before/after is critical. Use the same lighting, distance, and angle. The consistency makes the transformation undeniable.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Close-up of [problem area] under natural lighting, no filters"
- "Same exact camera angle and distance as the before shot"
- "Pointing at the transformation with genuine disbelief"

---

## 5. Day-in-Life

**Structure:** Product naturally integrated into daily routine

**Best for:** Lifestyle products, food/beverage, wellness, productivity apps

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: Morning routine establishing shot
2. CONTEXT: Normal daily activities, relatable setting
3. INTEGRATION: Product appears naturally — not as a presentation, as a habit
4. USAGE: Casual use while doing something else
5. CTA: Product sitting naturally in their environment (not centered or staged)

**Emotional arc:** Familiarity → recognition → aspiration

**Camera notes:** Multiple settings within familiar spaces. Less direct camera engagement — feels observed rather than performed. Slightly more movement and environmental variety.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Casually reaching for the product without looking at camera"
- "Product sitting naturally on [surface] among other everyday items"
- "Going about routine activities with product visible but not centered"

---

## 6. Tutorial/How-to

**Structure:** Step-by-step educational content

**Best for:** Makeup, cooking tools, tech products, DIY products

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: End result shown first (or a compelling question)
2. SETUP: Materials/product laid out
3. STEP 1-3: Clear demonstration with verbal-style pacing
4. RESULT: Final outcome
5. CTA: "Try it yourself" energy

**Emotional arc:** Curiosity → learning → accomplishment

**Camera notes:** Alternating between face-level (for explanations) and overhead/close-up (for demonstrations). Slower pacing than other archetypes.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Looking at camera while holding product, instructive but friendly expression"
- "Close-up of hands demonstrating the technique, steady framing"
- "Stepping back to reveal the finished result with a proud smile"

---

## 7. Reaction/Review

**Structure:** Genuine first-time reaction to the product

**Best for:** Food, surprising features, viral products, unexpected quality

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: Exaggerated curiosity or skepticism
2. SKEPTIC: "I didn't think this would work" energy
3. TRY: First use/taste/experience
4. REACTION: Genuine surprise (the money shot)
5. CTA: "OK I need to tell everyone about this"

**Emotional arc:** Skepticism → surprise → evangelism

**Camera notes:** Face must be clearly visible for reactions. Wide enough framing to capture body language. The reaction moment needs the most detailed prompt engineering.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Skeptical expression, one eyebrow raised, lips slightly pursed"
- "Eyes widening with genuine surprise, mouth slightly open"
- "Leaning toward camera with excited energy, gesturing emphatically"

---

## 8. GRWM (Get Ready With Me)

**Structure:** Product use during a getting-ready routine

**Best for:** Beauty, skincare, fashion, fragrance, wellness

**Scene flow:**
1. HOOK: Mirror shot or vanity setup
2. ROUTINE: Starting the getting-ready process
3. PRODUCT MOMENT: Natural integration of the featured product
4. APPLICATION: Detailed use moment
5. CTA: Final look with the product visible in frame

**Emotional arc:** Intimate → routine → polished → confident

**Camera notes:** Mirror reflections add authenticity. Soft, bathroom/vanity lighting. Close-ups during application. Final wide shot for the "finished" reveal.

**Key prompt phrases:**
- "Sitting at vanity/bathroom counter, natural morning lighting"
- "Applying product while looking in mirror, camera capturing reflection"
- "Final check in mirror, adjusting hair, satisfied with the result"

---

## Archetype Selection Guide

When the user does not specify an archetype, use this decision tree:

```
Is the product transformative (visible before/after)?
├── YES → Before/After
└── NO
    Is the product new or premium (high unboxing appeal)?
    ├── YES → Unboxing
    └── NO
        Does the product solve a specific pain point?
        ├── YES → Problem → Solution
        └── NO
            Is the product used in a routine?
            ├── YES → Day-in-Life or GRWM (beauty = GRWM, other = Day-in-Life)
            └── NO
                Is the product surprising or counterintuitive?
                ├── YES → Reaction/Review
                └── NO → Testimonial (safe default)
```
